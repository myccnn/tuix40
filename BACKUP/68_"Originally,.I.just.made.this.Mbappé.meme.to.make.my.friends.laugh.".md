# ["Originally, I just made this Mbappé meme to make my friends laugh."](https://github.com/myccnn/tuix40/issues/68)

**"Originally, I just made this Mbappé meme to make my friends laugh."
By creating a video montage of Kylian Mbappé set to Chinese communist music in the summer of 2023, he had no idea of ​​the storm he would unleash: Younes Khelifi, aka @frenchaicovers on X, is the Frenchman behind the infamous meme that sparked the controversy. For So Foot , he recounts his somewhat unusual last 24 hours.**


**How does it feel to have triggered [a geopolitical conflict with a meme that originated in your country three years ago](https://www.sofoot.com/articles/mao-zedong-le-dictateur-chinois-pourquoi-les-propos-de-kylian-mbappe-font-polemique-en-chine) ?**

(Laughs.) Honestly, I think I'm handling it pretty well. I already had some media exposure with that Twitter account back then, because I was one of the first to make deepfakes like that, so I'd already gotten some exposure for other memes. That's also why I decided to stop, because I felt it was interfering with my work. But it's true that now it's blown up so much… When I see it ending up on the 8 PM news, affecting La Liga's broadcasting rights, it's crazy. I don't feel bad, but it's strange to think that I'm partly responsible for all this. Originally, I just did this thing to make myself and my friends laugh, and I see it spreading and growing right before my eyes.


**What was going through your mind when you heard this story on Tuesday?**

I got up at 9:30 and grabbed my phone. I was chatting with some friends on Twitter, talking about soccer and sharing memes. And this particular meme has been popping up a lot on X for the past three years. I saw it in the conversation and thought, "It's just that tweet that resurfaced." And then, in the four or five shared messages, I started seeing "Chinese Foreign Minister , " "La Liga" ... (Laughs.) I was in bed, and I was really thinking, "This is crazy." So, five minutes later, I tweeted: "I wake up and see that I've caused a diplomatic incident." (Laughs.) Honestly, when I saw that, I wondered if there might be legal repercussions. Would the Mbappé camp sue me and demand compensation? But I did some research and I'm not too worried. My first thought was definitely what I put in the tweet: "If you sue me, Mbappé, I'll be financially ruined." (Laughs.) In any case, proportionally speaking, I think I'm worse off than him! Throughout the day, I watched the whole thing unfold, until this morning when I saw it ended up on the front page of L'Équipe . I went and bought four copies. (He laughs.)


**You still ended up on Gilles Bouleau's news program.**

Yes, when I started seeing the presenter with my meme that I made three years ago, displayed in 8k behind him… (He bursts into laughter.) _**We're in an episode of South Park , actually.**_


**Are your relatives aware that you are the one who started this?**

My mother doesn't know yet. At work, though, she does. They tell me, "What you've done is incredible, it's blowing up!" I'm going to tell my mother soon. I need to give her a call, but it's not easy to explain. (Laughs.)


**Did all this attract attention around you? We saw your tweet in Chinese welcoming your new followers.**

(Laughs.) No, honestly. And that tweet was half a joke. It was to reassure potential Chinese visitors, to show that I had sympathy for them and for China in general. It's really just a meme, a joke. It was also a way of humorously saying, "I didn't do anything, I'm just a kid, I have no worries." But if you're wondering, I haven't been contacted by an embassy or by the French or Chinese diplomatic corps. On the other hand, I would really like to go to China soon. I seriously wondered if I should contact the French embassy in China to make sure everything is OK regarding my meme and what just happened. I realize it's a bit of an unusual, even ridiculous, request, but I prefer to be sure. (Smiles.)


**You published this meme in the summer of 2023. Do you remember how you came up with the idea?**

At the time, there were rumors surrounding Mbappé's status in the PSG locker room, and people were saying he was an authority figure who laid down the law at the club. That's when I had the idea to combine that with the song "The Red Sun in the Sky" (a Chinese communist song dedicated to Mao Zedong, which became an internet meme in 2021). Honestly, it was just a flash of inspiration. I shared it on my Twitter account, and the video then started circulating widely elsewhere. I particularly remember that the Spanish media were very fond of content like that, especially around the time of the rumors about him leaving for Real Madrid.


**Had you already seen Mbappé's statement about your meme before?**

I found out about it like everyone else, on Monday evening. I even thought it was a statement made during the ceremony preparations. At first, I was really happy to see that he'd seen it; he even said it was his favorite meme about himself! After thinking about it, I realized, "The term 'Chinese dictator' could potentially be poorly received."  But I didn't think it would go beyond France... Honestly, I feel a bit sorry for him. No one really meant any harm, and yet it has consequences.


**After this story, do you think memes can have a real impact on what happens in real life?**

Yes, absolutely. I'd even say it's a crucial new form of communication for getting your voice heard. We saw it in Trump's campaigns: he greatly benefited from the use of memes featuring him to get re-elected, and it reached a whole new electorate. These days, a video can go viral to varying degrees and trigger a chain reaction. What just happened ended up reaching 1.4 billion people, and through a very high-profile figure, so the consequences are proportional. So yes, memes have a real impact. When a video is shared, it can get anywhere from 10 likes to 200,000 likes. It's something we can't control.


**One tweet summed up the situation quite well: "We live in a world where a random Frenchman, with a profile picture of Tung Tung Tung Sahur and basic editing skills, has provoked a conflict between a football star and the world's second-largest economic power."**

(Laughs.) That really made me laugh. Honestly, he said it all. I came up with it in 45 seconds, and look what happened. I wasn't working at the time, it was the middle of summer, I had nothing else to do. Viral things can't be controlled. It can really happen to anyone, like me.


**You've made a lot of memes about Mbappé in the past. Do you have any idea why he's such a popular figure on the internet?**

I've thought about it quite a bit, and I think it's a matter of perspective. As soon as he was in the spotlight in Monaco, he immediately became very serious in interviews and public appearances, even though he was very young. The contrast between his seriousness and his age quickly became apparent. He's from the 1998 generation like me, and he's still young. People my age might have thought, "Why is he so serious when he's just like us?  " He also has a certain tone in his speech: he'll always throw in a little jab, a joke, with a voice that puts people in their place. I think that plays a significant role as well.


**After this global exposure, do you want to relaunch @frenchaicovers? Or are you going to stay retired?**

(Laughs.) Honestly, I don't really feel like starting up again. I think I'll reuse my X account to give my perspective on what happened, since a lot of people have been speaking for me since yesterday. And I also want to take this opportunity to talk about my work; I'm a web developer, and I made these videos myself back when there wasn't any software. They were open-source projects that I reused, so it's somewhat related. But rest assured, I'm not going to start selling training courses or anything like that; I'm not into that. (Laughs.) These days, people make much funnier videos than mine. I've made my contribution, more than enough now.
